Reviving a Laser Microfabrication Station: Moving to a new motion controller

The stages of our laser microfabrication station stopped responding. I moved it to a new controller and brought it back to life.

  • Motion control
  • Newport XPS
  • Troubleshooting
  • Lab hardware

Our lab's laser microfabrication station moves the sample under the laser on Newport motorised stages: two linear stages for X and Y, a vertical stage for Z, and a rotary actuator axis (a PR50CC rotation stage) that turns the attenuator setting the laser power. A Newport XPS motion controller drives all four axes and opens and closes the laser shutter.

The motors stopped responding to the controller. I moved the whole system over to a second XPS controller, connected each stage to the driver card it needs, and updated the drivers and configuration until the software could drive the X, Y and Z stages, the rotary axis and the shutter again.

To make the next repair easier, I backed up both controllers and every configuration version, and put together a small kit with check, backup and restore scripts and the known-good configuration.
